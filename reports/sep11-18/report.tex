\section{Potential Function}

The obstacle potential is defined as

\begin{equation}
    U_{obst} = \frac{\lambda (\Delta v / \Delta v_{max})}
    {\left(1 - \dfrac{w}{\Delta x}\right)
    \sqrt{\left(\dfrac{\Delta x}{\tau(\Delta x, \Delta v_x)}\right)^2
    + \left(\dfrac{\Delta y}{\tau(\Delta y, \Delta v_y)}\right)^2}}
    \label{eq:potential}
\end{equation}

where the direction-dependent time constants are given by

\begin{align}
    \tau(\Delta x, \Delta v_x) &= \frac{(1 + \alpha \Delta v_x) + (\alpha \Delta v_x - 1)\tanh(-\Delta x \Delta v_x)}{2}\,\tau_x \\
    \tau(\Delta y, \Delta v_y) &= \frac{(1 + \alpha \Delta v_y) + (\alpha \Delta v_y - 1)\tanh(-\Delta y \Delta v_y)}{2}\,\tau_y
\end{align}

\noindent
Here $\Delta x, \Delta y$ are the relative position offsets to the obstacle vehicle, $\Delta v_x, \Delta v_y$ the relative velocities, $w$ the obstacle width, and $\lambda, \tau_x, \tau_y, \alpha, \Delta v_{max}$ are model parameters.

\section{Force at Fixed Points vs. Relative Velocity}

To examine the velocity dependence of the force in more detail, we fix the position and sweep the relative velocity, plotting the resulting force magnitude for several directions of $(\Delta v_x, \Delta v_y)$. We consider the point $(x, y) = (3, 30)$, i.e.\ ahead of and to the side of the obstacle, and the point $(x, y) = (-3, 30)$, on the opposite side.

\section{Removing the $\Delta v / \Delta v_{max}$ Term}

From Figure~\ref{fig:base_vel}, we observe that when the vehicle is receding, an increase in the relative velocity causes the force to increase as well. This is an undesirable effect, since a receding vehicle should not produce a growing repulsive force.

To avoid this behaviour, we drop the $\Delta v / \Delta v_{max}$ term from the original potential function in Equation~\ref{eq:potential}. Repeating the same two experiments with this modified potential gives the plot below.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering

    \caption{Force magnitude vs.\ relative velocity at $(x, y) = (3, 30)$ and $(x, y) = (-3, 30)$, with the $\Delta v / \Delta v_{max}$ term removed.}
    \label{fig:base_vel_f}
\end{figure}
